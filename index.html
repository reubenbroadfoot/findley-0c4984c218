What kindergarten instruction looks like

ELA uses Houghton Mifflin Into Reading (adopted May 2024) with explicit, systematic Structured Literacy in K–2 — decoding, fluency, spelling, grammar. Math learning targets for kindergarten center on counting to 100 and addition/subtraction concepts, using Contexts for Learning Mathematics and Dreambox. There is no mandated math homework at the district level.

The math department's advice to parents: play strategy games like Yahtzee, Blokus and Set for number sense rather than drilling speed, and let your child work Dreambox independently so the adaptive engine calibrates correctly.

4th grade

Fourth grade is the year TAG screening happens, OBOB opens up, and the Dragon Riders application lands in spring. Three things worth tracking.

TAG screening — happens automatically in October District process confirmed

Beaverton runs universal screening in grades 3, 4, and 5. Fourth grade takes the NGAT-Verbal, and testing is targeted for completion within October. You do not have to nominate your child to be tested.

  • Identification also weighs teacher observation (Kingore Inventory), classroom performance, and parent input. You can submit a parent referral form — it will not by itself trigger testing, but anecdotal evidence gets flagged to the building.
  • Beaverton does not accept private testing. If you have outside psychoeducational testing from elsewhere, it will not be used for identification.
  • Once identified, TAG status is permanent. Students can be retested every two years.
  • Identified students are not pulled into separate classes — the teacher builds an individualized plan with the family.
  • SUMMA, the district magnet program, is admission-by-test-score. Parents are notified in the fall by letter from the district office if their student is eligible to apply. No action needed to be considered.
  • Contact tag@beaverton.k12.or.us. Findley's own TAG page names facilitators who no longer appear in the staff directory — ask the office who currently holds the role.

Oregon Battle of the Books — act in September

Open to grades 3–5. This is the most parent-involvement-dependent thing on the fourth grade list, because students recruit their own teams.

  • Teams of 4–5 players, with a medieval-themed name to match the Dragons mascot.
  • Registration opens September 2026. Battles begin late January 2027, starting within grade level, then elimination rounds across grades. School champions advance to Regionals — a Findley 5th grade team made the Sweet Sixteen recently.
  • Run by Rita Vennes (library). The book list and signup form live on a Google Site that requires a district login, so ask her directly or watch ParentSquare.
  • Practical move: help your son or daughter line up teammates and start reading in August, before registration opens.

Dragon Riders — spring application

An enrichment program with per-trimester applications. Students apply for the first trimester in the spring of 4th grade, so this lands around February–April 2027 for your child.

  • 30 students accepted per trimester
  • Demand exceeds seats, so a lottery decides the final 30
  • Students must be in good standing in all classes

No application link, coordinator, or exact date is published. Ask about it at spring conferences.

Math Olympiad

Monthly contests November through March, 4th and 5th grade only. Five nonroutine problems, 30-minute limit, individual work, no calculators. Team capacity is around 35 students.

The PTO page dates to 2021 and lists no cost, registration window, or signup link. Email findleymatholympiad@gmail.com in September.

Passport Club — Levels 1 through 4

A volunteer-run geography program for grades 2–5. Students study monthly maps and get tested identifying world locations, earning a globe stamp in their passport plus a postage stamp "visa" per level. Fourth graders work Levels 1–4.

Checks are Wednesday mornings, roughly monthly October through May, with a second-chance day in late May. Testing happens in the technology room. Parents volunteer as "checkers" 8:30–10:30 am one Wednesday a month.

Health curriculum — read this one

The 4th grade scope and sequence of The Great Body Shop includes "Hormones and Body Changes" and "Hormones and Reproductive Organs." This is the puberty content.

Oregon lets you opt out of sexuality-education components by emailing the teacher and the principal, specifying which lessons. No deadline is published. You cannot opt out of the entire health curriculum without written religious or disability-related documentation. Erin's Law abuse-prevention material from fightchildabuse.org is also part of the curriculum, with preview and opt-out available.

Volunteering

One gate controls everything: the Raptor background check. Nothing else can happen until it clears.

The background check, step by step

  1. Complete the mandatory volunteer training first — training page. Do this before applying, not after.
  2. Apply through Raptor and authorize an Oregon criminal records check — English application · Spanish.
  3. Once approved, watch ParentSquare for signups and contact the activity or committee lead directly.
  • Takes up to 10 business days. The district will not expedite, even for an imminent field trip.
  • Valid two years, not one. You get an email reminder about 30 days before expiry.
  • Each volunteer needs a unique personal email. Shared household or couple addresses get rejected.
  • Confirmation emails come from raptortech.com and land in spam frequently.
  • Every visit, bring government-issued photo ID showing your date of birth. Staff scan it, run a sex-offender registry check, and print a badge.
  • Immigration status does not affect eligibility and an SSN is not required.
  • Field trips and chaperoning count as volunteering under district policy IICC, so full clearance is required — a same-week signup will not work.
  • Questions: volunteer@beaverton.k12.or.us · 503-356-4443. Denials or appeals: Public Safety, 503-356-4444.
If either of you was ever approved as a Beaverton volunteer before fall 2024, that approval was wiped when the district moved to Raptor. Everyone reapplied.

Meals — one open question

Beaverton uses SchoolCafe, not MySchoolBucks. Oregon has no universal free meals program, but the state's Expanded Income Guidelines extend free meals well above the federal cutoff — up to 300% of the federal poverty level — and any Oregon school that charges for meals must participate. Apply regardless of what you earn.

Some Beaverton schools operate under the federal Community Eligibility Provision, where every student eats free. I could not confirm whether Findley is one of them — worth one call to Nutrition Services at 503-356-3955. Ignore Findley's own meals page entirely; it still describes COVID-era curbside pickup.

Dragon Dash — the main fundraiser

A pledge-based run, roughly early October, with a donation window opening mid-September. Suggested goal $125 per student, tax receipts for donations of $25 and up. The registration link the PTO publishes (pledgestar.com/findley) does not currently load — checked Aug 10, 2026. The 2026 campaign link should come through ParentSquare.

Class-level prizes: the first class to 100% participation gets a short movie and extra recess. Merely registering counts toward class prizes — worth doing even if you donate nothing. Volunteering on event day requires background check clearance, and younger siblings cannot attend.

Weather closures

Decisions are posted by 5:30 am to the district website, Facebook, X, local media, School Messenger and ParentSquare. A two-hour delay means buses run snow routes; Findley is not on the no-service list. The district explicitly tells families to assess conditions at their own address and decide independently. There is no remote learning day policy.
